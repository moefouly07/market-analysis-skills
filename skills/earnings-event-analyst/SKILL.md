# Earnings Event Analyst

Analyze earnings as an expectations event, not simply a beat-or-miss event.

## Event setup

Establish the earnings date, announcement timing, relevant option expiry, and whether the event falls before or after expiration.

Use reliable available evidence for reported results, consensus estimates, company guidance, previous guidance, important margins, revenue quality, cash flow, capital expenditure, credible whisper estimates when available, pre-event price action, valuation, and positioning.

Never invent unavailable expectations, whisper estimates, or guidance details.

Separate company quality from stock reaction.

A company can report strong results and still fall. A company can miss estimates and still rise.

Do not assign one automatic explanation to a post-earnings move.

Consider guidance, margins, demand, revenue mix, cash flow, capex, valuation, positioning, prior price appreciation, macro conditions, and what appeared to be priced in.

Treat "priced in" as an interpretation unless observable market evidence supports it.

## Historical reaction

When historical earnings behavior is requested, use actual observations when available.

State the sample definition, sample size, and period.

Do not claim a reliable historical pattern from a tiny or selectively chosen sample.

Separate historical stock movement from a forecast for the current event.

When comparing reactions, distinguish close-to-close, after-hours, next-open, and next-session moves rather than mixing them.

## Options and event volatility

When options are involved, inspect the actual contract and inherit execution rules from `market-options-analyst` when that skill is available.

Evaluate event IV, market-implied expected move when available, expiry timing, liquidity, theta exposure, and possible post-event IV contraction.

Never assume constant IV across the event.

A correct directional call on the stock does not guarantee a profitable option trade.

If the main event occurs after the option expires, explicitly state that the contract does not directly capture that event.

Compare the move required for the option thesis with the expected move, recent earnings reactions, current technical structure, and DTE when reliable data is available.

## Expectations framework

Separate the following concepts whenever they materially differ:

reported result,
consensus expectation,
company guidance,
market-implied expectation,
valuation,
positioning,
stock reaction,
and option pricing.

A headline beat is not sufficient evidence that expectations were exceeded.

Do not infer weak demand merely from a selloff, and do not infer strong demand merely from a rally.

## Decision

Assess the fundamental result, expectations gap, stock-reaction setup, option structure, event timing, execution quality, and risk-reward separately.

For directional event analysis, give bullish, neutral, and bearish scenarios with falsifiable triggers, targets, and invalidations when the available data supports them.

Probability estimates are optional. Do not invent them merely to make the analysis appear quantitative.

If evidence is insufficient, say what remains unknown and what would materially change the conclusion.

Inherit all project-level data-integrity and output-format rules.
